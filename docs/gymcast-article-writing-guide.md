# Gymcast Resource Article Writing Guide

Use this document as the source of truth when planning, drafting, or reviewing a Gymcast resource guide.

## How to use this guide with ChatGPT

Upload this file and `gymcast-article-template.md` to a dedicated ChatGPT Project. Add the following Project instruction:

> Write Gymcast resource articles using the uploaded writing guide and article template. Follow the required structure, voice, and output format. Do not invent product capabilities, statistics, customer results, prices, or sources. Flag missing information with `[NEEDS INPUT: ...]`. Return a complete draft followed by the publishing details and final checklist.

For each article, provide ChatGPT with:

- The working title or target search query.
- The intended reader and their main problem.
- The article's goal and desired next action.
- Any facts, product details, internal links, and approved sources it may use.
- The target length, if it differs from the default in this guide.

## Purpose of a Gymcast resource guide

A resource guide should answer a practical question for a gym owner, operator, coach, or member of staff. It should help the reader make a decision or complete a task, while naturally showing where Gymcast may make gym display management easier.

The article must be useful without requiring the reader to buy Gymcast. It should explain genuine alternatives and trade-offs before presenting dedicated software as an appropriate option.

## Audience

Write primarily for busy gym owners and operators. The reader is perfectly capable of using technology, but they do not work in technology. Never talk down to them and never assume they use technical vocabulary.

Assume the reader knows how to:

- Use their phone.
- Create a slide or presentation.
- Put a file onto a USB stick and plug it into a TV.
- Open and use Netflix or another app on a Smart TV.

Do not assume the reader knows:

- That the app used to open websites is called a browser.
- What terms such as file format or file type mean.
- The difference between casting and mirroring.
- What cloud storage means or how it works.
- Why a Smart TV cannot normally open a PowerPoint file directly.

Use the actions above as familiar points of reference. For example, say “open the website using the internet app on your TV” before introducing the word “browser”. Explain “cloud storage” as “files stored online so you can open them from different devices”.

The reader usually wants to know:

- What their realistic options are.
- Which option suits the size and complexity of their gym.
- What equipment, setup, or staff effort each option requires.
- What can go wrong in day-to-day use.
- When a centrally managed system is worth considering.

## Voice and style

- Use clear, practical British English.
- Sound informed, calm, and helpful rather than promotional.
- Address the reader as “you” and refer to “your gym” where natural.
- Prefer short sentences, short paragraphs, and concrete examples.
- Prefer everyday words: use “show”, “send”, “open”, “save”, “screen”, and “internet connection” when they communicate the meaning accurately.
- Describe what the reader needs to do before naming the technology involved.
- Explain every necessary technical term in plain language on first use. Do not introduce a technical term when the reader can complete the task without it.
- When two similar terms matter, explain the visible difference. For example: “Mirroring shows everything from your phone on the TV. Casting sends one video or app to the TV, so you can continue using your phone.”
- Explain device limitations rather than presenting them as obvious. For example, a Smart TV may have apps but usually does not include the Microsoft software needed to open a PowerPoint file.
- Use familiar comparisons only when they make an instruction clearer, such as comparing a TV app with opening Netflix.
- Focus on operational consequences: staff time, reliability, scheduling, screen locations, and ease of updating content.
- Make recommendations conditional on the reader's circumstances.
- Give the direct answer early, then explain the reasoning.
- Use descriptive headings that make sense when scanned on their own.

Avoid:

- Hype, fear, or aggressive sales language.
- Empty phrases such as “in today's fast-paced world”, “game-changer”, or “revolutionise”.
- Unsupported superlatives such as “best”, “leading”, or “perfect”.
- Presenting Gymcast as the answer before explaining the reader's needs.
- Repeating the same keyword unnaturally.
- Unexplained terms such as browser, file format, casting, mirroring, cloud storage, HDMI input, media player, content management system, or digital signage platform.
- Instructions that name a feature without saying where the reader is likely to find it or what it will do.
- Phrases such as “simply” or “just” when a step may be unfamiliar.
- Long scene-setting introductions.
- Claims about compatibility, integrations, pricing, performance, or results unless supplied and approved.
- Invented quotations, statistics, case studies, or citations.

## Required article structure

### 1. WordPress post title

Supply one clear title outside the article body. WordPress renders the post title, so do not add an H1 inside the body.

The title should:

- Describe the question or decision the guide resolves.
- Include the main topic naturally.
- Set an accurate expectation for the article.
- Usually stay below 65 characters when that can be done without making it vague.

### 2. Article introduction

The WordPress Gymcast Resource Guide pattern supplies the visual wrapper. Provide these values:

- **Kicker:** Always `Gymcast Resource Guide`.
- **Standfirst:** One or two sentences summarising what the reader will learn and why it is useful. Aim for 25–45 words.
- **Best for:** A specific description of the intended reader.
- **Reading time:** Estimate from the finished draft, using roughly 200 words per minute and rounding up.
- **Last updated:** The current month and year at publication, not at initial drafting.

Do not repeat the full title in the standfirst. Move quickly to the reader's practical need.

### 3. Main article content

Use H2 headings for every top-level section. The site automatically builds the contents list from H2 headings inside the Main Article Content group, so:

- Do not write a manual contents section.
- Keep every main H2 unique and descriptive.
- Put only genuine article sections inside the main-content area.
- Do not include the FAQ, Related Guides, or final CTA as main-content H2s.

Use H3 headings only where they help divide a substantial H2 section. Do not skip heading levels.

Most guides should contain four to seven H2 sections and follow this editorial flow:

1. **Direct answer or why the decision matters.** Establish the practical issue and give the reader an early orientation.
2. **Decision criteria.** Explain what changes the answer: gym size, number of screens, room layout, timetable complexity, connectivity, budget, and staff time, as relevant.
3. **Options or recommended approach.** Give each meaningful option its own H2, or organise a how-to guide into clear sequential sections.
4. **Trade-offs and operational considerations.** Explain setup, maintenance, reliability, and limitations rather than listing features alone.
5. **When dedicated gym display software makes sense.** Include this only when relevant. Describe observable circumstances, not a generic sales pitch.
6. **Recommendation or next steps.** Help the reader match the available approaches to their situation.

Adapt the wording and number of sections to the topic. Do not use generic headings such as “Option 1” in a finished article.

### 4. Comparison table, when useful

Use a comparison table when readers are choosing between three or more genuine options. Use these columns unless the topic needs a better set:

| Option | Best for | Advantages | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specific option | Suitable situation | Concise practical benefits | Costs, limitations, or work required |

Keep cells concise. Explain important nuance in the surrounding prose. Do not create a table merely to repeat paragraphs.

### 5. FAQ topics

The site FAQ section is dynamic and draws answers from separate WordPress FAQ posts. Do not place FAQ headings and answers in the main article body.

Instead, suggest up to four FAQ questions after the draft. Prefer questions that:

- Closely match the article's search intent.
- Have short, self-contained answers.
- Resolve a genuine concern not already answered clearly in the main body.
- Can be reused across closely related guides.

Mark each suggestion as either an existing FAQ to select or a proposed new FAQ to create. Never assume an FAQ exists unless its link or title was provided.

### 6. Related guides

The Related Guides section is dynamic and displays up to three published guides. Do not add this section to the article body.

After the draft, recommend up to three closely related internal articles. Use only supplied or verified URLs. If the relevant article does not yet exist, label it as a content opportunity instead of inventing a URL.

Also suggest a small set of relevant WordPress tags. Shared tags help the site select related guides and FAQs automatically, so use consistent, specific topic labels rather than one-off variations.

### 7. Final CTA

The WordPress pattern adds the standard CTA after Related Guides. Do not include another CTA in the main article body unless the brief explicitly requires one.

Standard CTA:

> **Want an Easier Way to Manage Gym TV Displays?**
>
> Gymcast helps gyms automatically display the right class on the right screen at the right time.
>
> **Button:** Book a Demo  
> **Link:** `/book-demo/`

Only change this copy when an approved article-specific CTA is supplied.

## Writing the main sections

Each main section should do at least one of the following:

- Answer a question.
- Explain a decision factor.
- Compare viable approaches.
- Give an actionable step.
- Warn about a meaningful limitation.

Open a section with its most useful point. Follow with explanation, practical detail, and an example where helpful. Use bullet points for genuinely parallel items, not as a substitute for connected reasoning.

When comparing options, cover the same criteria for each option where possible. Useful criteria include:

- Initial equipment or setup.
- Ongoing staff effort.
- Suitability for one screen versus several screens.
- Ability to show different content in different rooms.
- Scheduling and frequency of changes.
- Internet or local network dependency.
- Reliability and recovery when something goes wrong.
- Likely limitations as the gym grows.

## Product mentions

Mention Gymcast only where it helps answer the reader's question. A useful product mention connects a real operational problem to a verified capability.

Do not:

- Invent features or integrations.
- Claim that Gymcast works on every television or device.
- State or imply guaranteed business outcomes.
- disparage alternative products or manual approaches.
- Turn every section into a product pitch.

If the brief does not contain enough verified product information, keep the discussion category-level (“dedicated gym display software”) and leave the standard CTA to introduce Gymcast.

## Accuracy and sourcing

- Treat supplied Gymcast documentation as the authority for product claims.
- Verify time-sensitive facts such as prices, device support, software features, and regulations before publication.
- Link to primary sources for technical or factual claims where possible.
- Distinguish facts from recommendations.
- Never fabricate a source or URL.
- Use `[NEEDS INPUT: describe the missing fact]` when required information is unavailable.

## SEO and publishing details

After the article, provide:

- **Suggested URL slug:** Short, lowercase, and hyphenated.
- **Meta title:** Accurate and compelling; aim for approximately 50–60 characters.
- **Meta description:** A natural summary with a clear benefit; aim for approximately 140–160 characters.
- **Primary search topic:** One phrase, used naturally rather than mechanically.
- **Secondary topics:** Closely related phrases and questions covered by the article.
- **Suggested tags:** A concise, reusable set for matching guides and FAQs.
- **FAQ selections or opportunities:** Up to four.
- **Related guide selections or opportunities:** Up to three.

These are publishing notes, not part of the visible article body.

## Images and accessibility

Where an image, diagram, or screenshot would materially help, insert a note in this form:

`[IMAGE: what the image should show | ALT: concise description of its informative content]`

Do not add decorative-image placeholders. Alt text should describe the useful content of the image, not repeat the article title or begin with “Image of”.

## Default length

Use the shortest length that answers the topic properly. As a working default, aim for 1,000–1,600 words of main article content. A narrow question may need less; a detailed comparison may need more. Do not pad a draft to reach a word count.

## Required ChatGPT output order

Return drafts in this order:

1. Article setup details.
2. Main article body in Markdown, beginning with the standfirst and excluding an H1.
3. Publishing details.
4. FAQ selections or opportunities.
5. Related guide selections or opportunities.
6. Assumptions and `[NEEDS INPUT]` items.
7. Completed quality checklist.

## Final quality checklist

Before returning a draft, confirm that:

- [ ] The post title answers a clear search intent and is not repeated as an H1 in the body.
- [ ] The standfirst explains the value of the guide in one or two sentences.
- [ ] “Best for”, reading time, and last-updated details are supplied.
- [ ] The direct answer appears early.
- [ ] H2 headings are unique, descriptive, and suitable for the automatic contents list.
- [ ] The draft uses H3 only beneath an H2 and does not skip heading levels.
- [ ] Options are assessed using consistent, practical criteria.
- [ ] Recommendations explain who each approach suits and why.
- [ ] Any comparison table adds information rather than duplicating prose.
- [ ] Product claims are verified and Gymcast is not over-promoted.
- [ ] No facts, statistics, quotations, sources, or URLs were invented.
- [ ] FAQ, Related Guides, and CTA blocks were not written into the main body.
- [ ] FAQ and related-guide recommendations are supplied separately.
- [ ] British English, short paragraphs, and plain language are used consistently.
- [ ] Necessary technical terms are explained using familiar actions, and unnecessary technical terms are removed.
- [ ] Instructions do not assume the reader understands browsers, file formats, casting, mirroring, cloud storage, or Smart TV file support.
- [ ] Publishing details and any missing-input flags are included.
